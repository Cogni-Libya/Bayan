Not available, or not what they are often described as (checked 2026-09-17):
- **MultiSim** (GitHub `XenonMolecule/MultiSim`, HF `MichaelR207/MultiSim`) ships no Arabic data — its only
  Arabic entry, Saaq al-Bambuu, is private. There is **no Simple Arabic Wikipedia**.
- **ATSC** is downloadable from GitHub `AMahfodh/ArSummarizer`, path `TSimAr/resources/Simplification_Datasets(ATSC)`
  (no license file — ask the authors before redistributing).
- **Arabic EW-SEW / WikiLarge** repo (`SuhaSaleh/Arabic-EW-SEW-and-WikiLarge-datasets`) contains only
  ~4,000 / 400 / 50 EW-SEW lines and a small WikiLarge test file — not the paper's 82,585 pairs. No license file.
- **Al-Safy** (`laithjalamneh/Al-Safy-Arabic-Text-Simplification`, MIT) contains training code only; its
  "9,185 pairs" are 4,307 legal/news pairs expanded by synonym augmentation, and the data is not published.
- **mEdIT** (`grammarly/medit`, CC-BY-NC-4.0) has Arabic grammatical-error-correction rows but no Arabic
  simplification rows.

| Work | Verified content | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **AraT5-MSAizer** — Fares, OSACT @ LREC-COLING 2024, pp. 124–129. [doi:10.63317/26nxp6cqn8q2](https://doi.org/10.63317/26nxp6cqn8q2) | Fine-tunes AraT5v2 for dialect → MSA translation | Precedent for fine-tuning AraT5v2 on an Arabic-to-Arabic rewriting task. |

| **BALSAM** — Al-Matham et al., ArabicNLP 2025. [arXiv:2507.22603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22603) | Arabic LLM benchmark: 78 tasks, 14 categories, ~52K examples; includes a **Text Simplification** task (98 test / 41 dev in Phase 1) | Could provide extra Arabic simplification test items and LLM comparisons. |
| **RL for Catalan simplification** — Ayguadé Domingo, Bott & Saggion, 2026. [arXiv:2609.04823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04823) | GRPO post-training of IberianLLM-7B on ASSET with a SARI-based reward plus a length penalty; improved on Catalan benchmarks, but cross-lingual transfer (translated ASSET) showed no significant out-of-domain gain | An idea for later, not evidence for Arabic. |
| **Can LLMs control readability? (Arabic)** — Rabih, Qwaider & Briscoe, READIxTSAR @ LREC 2026. [arXiv:2606.21981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21981) | For CEFR-targeted Arabic generation, **unconstrained prompting gives weak level control**; CEFR-guided prompting **with lexical constraints** reached 0.99 agreement with predicted readability and 0.91 similarity to reference linguistic profiles | Put explicit lexical constraints (e.g. SAMER-lexicon levels) into Bayan's generator prompt instead of only "simplify to easy". |
| **Policy-based sentence simplification** — Wu, Arase & Nagata, 2025. [arXiv:2512.06228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06228) | Uses LLM-as-a-judge to build policy-aligned training data with **no parallel corpus or human annotation**; small open models (Phi-3-mini-3.8B) surpassed GPT-4o on lexical-oriented simplification and matched it on full rewriting | Closest published precedent for Bayan's data pipeline; also shows the *policy* (lexical-only vs. full rewrite) should be chosen explicitly. |
| **Step-by-step proficiency-controlled simplification** — Zhang et al., Findings of EACL 2026. [arXiv:2602.07499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07499) | LLMs are limited at simplifying **across large readability gaps**; decomposing into steps improves control, but meaning preservation during extensive simplification remains unsolved, and human annotators disagree on meaning-preservation judgments | Large jumps (hard → easy) are the riskiest; consider stepwise targets and budget for meaning checks. |
| **Taming CATS (control tokens)** — Hubarava & Gao, READIxTSAR @ LREC 2026. [ACL Anthology 2026.readi-1.3](https://aclanthology.org/2026.readi-1.3/) | Instruction fine-tuning with discrete control tokens learned readability control consistently (English; Llama/Mistral/Qwen 1–14B); small models were competitive; **the training-data distribution largely determines controllability** | Bayan's level tokens only work if training pairs actually differ across levels — Baseet's outputs often do not. |
| **LLMs across simplification tasks** — Qiang et al., 2025. [arXiv:2502.08281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08281) | LLMs outperformed non-LLM methods on lexical, syntactic, sentence and document simplification, and often **exceeded the quality of human-annotated references** | Benchmark zero-shot LLMs against AraT5 honestly; reference-based scores can penalize outputs better than the references. |
| **Arabic GEC with synthetic data** — Dekmak, AUB thesis, 2026 ([scholarworks.aub.edu.lb](https://scholarworks.aub.edu.lb/items/4728dcd0-668f-419f-ba03-8c1749e69d6a)) | AraT5v2-base-1024 trained on ~650K distilled synthetic pairs reached 80.86 F0.5 on QALB; 3–14B LLMs underperformed specialized models, with the gap narrowing as scale grows; data quality was the main driver | The closest Arabic evidence that "AraT5v2 + LLM-distilled synthetic data" works for an Arabic rewriting task (grammar correction, not simplification). |
| **Error-based human evaluation of LLMs in sentence simplification** — Wu & Arase, ACM, July 2025. [arXiv:2403.04963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04963) · [doi:10.1145/3744744](https://doi.org/10.1145/3744744) | Human error annotation on Turk/ASSET/Newsela/SimPA. Total errors: Qwen2.5-72B **172**, GPT-4 **211**, Llama-3.2-3B **326**, fine-tuned Control-T5 **350**. Altered Meaning (lexical): Control-T5 **176** vs GPT-4 94, Qwen 59. Llama-3.2-3B, the only small model, showed **notably more Repetition and Hallucination**. Automatic metrics were found insufficiently sensitive to rank high-quality simplifications | **The most directly relevant evidence Bayan has, and it contradicts the project brief's architecture argument**: the fine-tuned encoder-decoder made the most meaning errors of any system. Supports only the size-conditioned claim — small decoder-only models are the risky ones, while LLMs that beat seq2seq are far too large to ship. See §1.1 of `architecture_decisions.md`. |
| **SALSA: edit-level simplification evaluation** — Heineman, Dou, Maddela & Xu, EMNLP 2023. [2023.emnlp-main.211](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.211/) | 19K human edit annotations on 840 simplifications from 11 models + 2 humans, 21 edit types. 16% of all model edits are errors (best model few-shot GPT-3.5 at 9%, humans 6%); **63% of simplifications contain at least one error**. Fine-tuned **MUSS** (controllable BART-large) "produce[s] more diverse edits than GPT-3.5, yet suffer[s] from **incredibly high errors**"; fine-tuned **T5-3B/11B** instead "learn to **minimize loss by making very few changes**". Open instruction-tuned models (Alpaca, Vicuna) match GPT-3.5's edit count "**at a cost of more conceptual errors due to the inherent limits of model imitation**" | Second independent confirmation that a fine-tuned encoder-decoder is not the faithful option. Also the clearest published statement of Bayan's two failure modes: **copying** (T5 minimising loss) and **high error rates** (MUSS) — and evidence that imitating a larger teacher has limits, which bears directly on Bayan's distillation plan. |
| **Small LLMs for on-device lexical simplification** — Hayakawa, Bott & Saggion, **INLG 2025**. [arXiv:2509.25086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25086) | Five languages; small locally-deployable LLMs (~1–3B), in-context learning vs knowledge distillation, automatic **and manual** evaluation classifying outputs as Unchanged / Beneficial / **Harmful**. Findings: small models' "limited capacity compared to larger counterparts introduces critical considerations regarding the reliability and harmfulness of the generated simplifications"; **knowledge distillation raises automatic scores but increases the proportion of Harmful outputs**; 5-shot small models instead produce many Unchanged outputs (safe but useless); **output log-probability is an effective signal for detecting harmful simplifications**, enabling a filtering strategy that suppresses them while keeping most beneficial ones | The closest paper to Bayan's actual deployment situation — on-device, privacy-motivated, vulnerable users, and it cites Rello et al. (2013) on dyslexia as Bayan does. Two direct consequences: (1) **distillation is a safety risk, not just a quality risk** — it trades harm for metric gains; (2) a cheap, implementable safety gate exists (log-probability filtering) that needs no extra model. Argues Bayan's runtime faithfulness gate should be core, not a stretch goal. |
| **Fine-tuning vs prompting at sub-1B scale** — Cohen, Bul, Inbar & Loewenbach (Tel Aviv Univ.), 2026. [arXiv:2601.05794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05794) | Encoder-decoders under 1B (BART base/large, T5 base/large, Flan-T5 base/large, Pegasus, ProphetNet) on ASSET, Med-EASi, OneStopEnglish. Fine-tuning gives consistently higher SARI; **prompting scores higher on BERTScore/LENS mainly by copying the input** (identical ratio > 0.50 in many cases). Human raters preferred fine-tuned outputs **46.9% vs 31.5%** (p = 0.0062) | Supports Bayan's plan at exactly its scale: at sub-1B, fine-tuning beats prompting, and semantic-similarity metrics reward trivial copying. Reinforces the decision to early-stop on SARI rather than loss or BERTScore. |
| **Arabic GEC system comparison** — Alrehili & Alhothali (King Abdulaziz Univ. / Saudi Electronic Univ.), 2025. [arXiv:2511.14230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14230) | ArbESC+ edit-selection system combination; its Table 1 reports the individual systems on QALB. **AraT5**: F0.5 80.09 (QALB-2014 L1) / 80.06 (2015 L1) / 64.22 (2015 L2). **AraBART**: 78.17 / 77.97 / 60.46. GPT-4 (numbers taken from Kwon et al., 2023) reached F0.5 **67** on QALB-2014. Over-correction analysed in §8.1.3, with overcounting ratios above 95% for some categories | Independent Arabic measurements on a faithfulness-critical rewriting task: a fine-tuned Arabic seq2seq beats a large decoder-only LLM by ~13 F0.5, and AraT5 leads AraBART by 1.9–3.8 F0.5 at 2.6× the parameters. |
| **ChatGPT over-corrects in GEC** — Fang et al., 2023. [arXiv:2304.01746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01746) | ChatGPT detects errors well and produces very fluent corrections, but **over-corrects and does not adhere to the principle of minimal edits**. Coyne et al., 2023 ([arXiv:2303.14342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14342)) likewise find GPT-3.5/GPT-4 score highly on the fluency-oriented JFLEG benchmark but not on minimal-edit data | Measured evidence for "assistant drift": a decoder-only model rewrites more than it was asked to. That is the failure mode Bayan must avoid, where a looser rewrite silently changes meaning for a vulnerable reader. |
| **Over-correction survives fine-tuning** — Park, Do & Lee, 2025. [arXiv:2509.20811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20811) | On the BEA-19 test set: fine-tuned Chat-LLaMA-2-7B/13B reach precision 72.3 / 74.6 (F0.5 71.2 / 73.1), while a **770M T5-large** system reaches precision **78.0** and F0.5 **75.7** | Fine-tuning does not remove the decoder-only over-editing tendency, and a far smaller encoder-decoder was the more precise editor. Supports Bayan's architecture choice with numbers rather than intuition. |
| **Factuality errors in simplification** — Devaraj, Sheffield, Wallace & Li, ACL 2022. [2022.acl-long.506](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.506/) | Taxonomy of information **insertion, deletion and substitution** errors. Errors appear in both system outputs and the reference corpora themselves (Newsela, WikiLarge); deletion errors are frequent and models learn the behaviour from the data; existing metrics do not capture them | The counterweight to the argument above: encoder-decoder simplifiers also hallucinate and drop information. Bayan's faithfulness claim must rest on its own human meaning check, not on an architecture argument. |
| **HPLT 3.0 monolingual encoder-decoders** — LREC 2026. [arXiv:2511.01066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01066) · [2026.lrec-1.110](https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.110/) | 57 language-specific T5-base models including Arabic (`HPLT/hplt_t5_base_3_0_ara_Arab`, Apache 2.0). Same T5-base body as AraT5v2 (12+12 layers, d_model 768) but vocab **32,768** vs AraT5v2's 110,208. Reported to outperform mT5-base on NER and MultiBLIMP | A structurally smaller Arabic encoder-decoder candidate (~230M vs 368M) with no capacity sacrifice. **No generation or Arabic rewriting benchmarks are published**, so it is untested for simplification. |
| **LLM data generation for low-resource languages** — Anikina et al., EMNLP 2025. [arXiv:2506.12158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12158) | Across 11 languages, **target-language demonstrations plus LLM-based revision** gave the best synthetic data, narrowing the gap to real data to as little as ~5% in some settings | Add Arabic few-shot examples and a revision pass to the generator. |
| **YallaMorph** — Reda, Khalifa, Marzouk & Habash, 2026. [arXiv:2609.10153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10153) | Benchmark for Arabic morphological generation in LLMs | Morphological errors in LLM-generated simplifications (title-level check). |
| **DSPy** — Khattab et al., 2023. [arXiv:2310.03714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03714) | Framework for compiling LM pipelines against metrics | Used for Bayan's generator and equivalence validator. |

## 5. Corrections to earlier versions of these references

- SAMER is 15 novels with Levels 5 → 4 → 3; it is **not** "Saaq al-Bambuu" and has no "Full/Partial/Lexical" levels. No fine-tuned AraT5 results exist in the SAMER paper.
- The BAREC Shared Task winner is !MSA, not "GNNinjas". BAREC's authors do not include Alhafni.
- ATSC is 500 documents / 546 → 1,243 sentences, not "2,042 sentences".
- TSimAr is 2023 with SARI 0.73 (not "2025, SARI 39.8"); ATSimST scored SARI 0.81 (not "~0.75+" or "41.2"), and there is no "40% training-latency reduction" claim.
- Khallaf et al. reported no SARI or BLEU; any such numbers attributed to them are invented.
- HF IDs `aubmindlab/arat5-base`, `aubmindlab/arat5v2-base-1024` and `CAMeL-Lab/BAREC` do not exist; the model is `UBC-NLP/AraT5v2-base-1024`. GitHub `tareknaous/readme` is ReadMe++, not MultiSim.
