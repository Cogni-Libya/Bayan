{\color{sicblue}\bfseries\large Abstract}
\end{center}
\begin{quote}\small
Arabic places barriers in front of readers with dyslexia that tools built for
Latin scripts do not address: short vowels are omitted, clitics attach directly
to the stem, and clauses chain without sentence-final punctuation. We present
Bayan, an Android plugin that simplifies Arabic text in place. Selecting text in
any application offers \ar{تبسيط} (\emph{Simplify}) in the system text-selection
menu; simplification, diacritization and read-aloud all run on the device, so the
text never leaves the phone. This report covers the work completed at the
two-thirds point of a three-week capstone. Our central methodological finding is
that Arabic simplification corpora contain so many sentences needing no change
that a model doing nothing scores well: on the SAMER test split, copying the
input yields SARI 77.5 overall but 56.1 on the rows a human actually simplified.
Reported against that baseline, our first fine-tuned model gains $+2.8$ SARI
overall and $+7.5$ where the work is --- and \emph{loses} to the baseline under
the greedy decoding we ship on-device. We also detect and remove 9.3\% test-set
contamination in a published Arabic simplification corpus before training on it.
Every claim below is labelled \measured, \decided\ or \planned.
\end{quote}

% ============================================================== 1 Introduction
\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background Information}

Dyslexia impairs reading through phonological processing. Modern Standard Arabic
compounds this in three specific ways.

\textbf{Omitted short vowels.} Written Arabic normally leaves short vowels
unmarked, so one orthographic form carries several readings: \ar{كتب} is
\emph{kataba} (he wrote), \emph{kutiba} (it was written) or \emph{kutub} (books).
The reader resolves this from context --- precisely the process dyslexia impairs.

\textbf{Agglutination.} Arabic is templatic and agglutinative. Conjunctions,
articles and object pronouns attach directly to the stem, producing long
unbroken words that crowd the line and resist chunking.

\textbf{Clause chaining.} Prose chains clauses with \ar{و} and \ar{ف} across long
stretches without sentence-final punctuation, so a single sentence can exceed
working memory.

Full diacritization is not a complete answer: marking every character adds
visual clutter of its own. The useful intervention is to simplify first, then
vocalise. We found no deployed Arabic dyslexia-support software and no large
open Arabic simplification corpus, which shapes both the product and the data
work below.

\subsection{Motivation and Objective}

\textbf{Objective.} Let an Arabic reader with dyslexia read difficult text
independently, wherever they meet it.

\decided The product is an Android plugin registered on the system
\texttt{PROCESS\_TEXT} intent, so \ar{تبسيط} (\emph{Simplify}) appears in the
text-selection menu of every application. The reader selects text in a message,
a news page or a PDF and is helped in place. A standalone application was
rejected for the opposite reason: it reintroduces the copy-and-paste friction
the project exists to remove.

\decided Every model runs on the device. The reader's text never leaves the
phone, the tool works offline, and no hosted service sits on the critical path
of the final demonstration. \autoref{fig:arch} shows the arrangement.

\subsection{Training Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\measured \textbf{Model~1.} AraT5v2-base-1024 \cite{arat5}, 368M parameters,
fine-tuned on SAMER L5$\rightarrow$L3. Learning rate $10^{-4}$, AdamW with weight
decay 0.01, 100 warmup steps, effective batch 16, fp16, gradient checkpointing,
10 epochs over 3{,}250 steps, seed 42. Identity pairs capped at 20\% in training
and left untouched in dev and test.

Three corrections follow from its measured behaviour and apply to every later
run.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Select on the changed-reference subset, decoded greedily.}
Selecting on full-dev SARI pays a model for inaction, because roughly half the
dev set needs no change; selecting under beam search measures a decoder we do
not ship.
\item \textbf{Stop on validation loss, not SARI.} Validation loss bottoms near
step 2{,}200 while SARI wanders inside noise for a further 1{,}400 steps
(\autoref{fig:training}) and picked its checkpoint on a 0.18 difference.
\item \textbf{Train on plain and diacritized copies of every pair.} SAMER
carries no diacritics at all, and the resulting model degrades badly on
diacritized input (\autoref{fig:ood}).
\end{enumerate}

\planned \textbf{Model~2.} The same recipe over a mixed corpus of
$\approx$107{,}000 pairs across three domains, with level tokens so one model
offers three simplification strengths plus a leave-unchanged mode. AraBART
\cite{arabart} (139M) is trained alongside AraT5v2 on identical data; the choice
between them will be made on measured results, with the smaller model preferred
at equal quality because it makes on-device decoding affordable.

\subsection{Workflow}

\autoref{fig:pipeline} shows the path from corpus to score. Two gates decide
what may be trained on: a human review of every LLM-generated pair, and a
leakage check against the locked test sets. Only the evaluation lead runs the
locked sets, and only on final models; everyone else tunes on dev.

\subsection{System Design}

One Android application, two activities, three models.
\texttt{ProcessTextActivity} registers
\texttt{android.intent.action.PROCESS\_TEXT} with mime type
\texttt{text/plain}. A translucent theme lets the result float over the host
application, which avoids requesting the \texttt{SYSTEM\_ALERT\_WINDOW} overlay
permission entirely --- a meaningful reduction in what the user must grant.
Inference runs behind a \texttt{TextSimplifier} interface so the engine can be
replaced without touching the interface; decoding is greedy and the tokenizer is
exported inside the ONNX graph, so no Python runtime is needed on the phone.

\decided Size budget: 250\,MB for the simplifier plus $\approx$45\,MB for
diacritization and read-aloud. Vocabulary-pruned int8 is $\approx$223\,MB and
fits; int4 ($\approx$112\,MB) is the fallback if load time or memory on a
mid-range phone proves binding. Models download on first run rather than
shipping inside the APK.

% ================================================================== 3 Results
\section{Results}

\subsection{Data Preprocessing}

\textbf{Normalisation for comparison only.} Diacritics and punctuation are
stripped and alef variants (\ar{أ إ آ}), \ar{ة}/\ar{ه} and \ar{ى}/\ar{ي}
unified. This is used for leakage detection and scoring alignment, never on
training text, where the original orthography is preserved.

\textbf{Test sets locked first.} Two splits were locked before any training and
are never trained on: BAREC test (7{,}286 rows) and SAMER test (3{,}277 rows),
each recorded by row count and SHA-256 in a committed manifest. Both are the
official upstream splits, unmodified.

\measured \textbf{Leakage filtering.} Every training file is checked against both
locked sets for exact matches and near-duplicates, using character 5-gram
Jaccard overlap at a 0.8 threshold. Matches shorter than five words are reported
but not counted, because short formulaic fragments recur in any corpus.

\keynote{This check found that \textbf{3{,}190 of Baseet's 34{,}294 rows (9.3\%)}
overlapped our locked test sets --- 1{,}829 touching SAMER test and 1{,}361
touching BAREC test. Put the other way, \textbf{1{,}835 of SAMER's 3{,}933
substantive test sentences (47\%) appear somewhere in that corpus}. Training on
it unfiltered would have produced inflated scores with no error and nothing to
notice. The rows were removed and the remainder re-verified clean.}

\subsection{Exploratory Data Analysis}

The corpora differ in ways that turned out to drive model behaviour, so we
report them rather than assume them.

\textbf{SAMER is literary and short.} Drawn from early twentieth-century novels;
the test split has median sentence length 6 words, and 17.8\% of rows are three
words or fewer --- chapter headings and dialogue fragments rather than sentences.
48.8\% of test rows are identity pairs. It splits \emph{by chapter, not by
novel}, so all fifteen novels appear in all three splits: its test set is fully
in-domain and SARI on it reads optimistically. It contains \textbf{no diacritics
at all}.

\textbf{BAREC duplicates itself.} 311 sentences appear in both its train and
test splits, and 797 of 7{,}286 test rows are two words or fewer. Both cause
false alarms in leakage checking and motivate the minimum-length floor. 56\% of
its sentences carry diacritics.

\textbf{Baseet is closer to the product.} Median source length 14 words, 35.4\%
diacritized, no identity pairs at any level; its strongest level shortens a
14-word median source to 9 words. \textbf{DAASI} is government and insurance
text, median 12 words, 32\% diacritized, no identity pairs.

\subsection{Modeling}
\label{sec:modeling}

\measured \textbf{The copy baseline comes first.} On a corpus where half the
sentences need no change, a model that does nothing scores well, so every result
is reported beside copying the input. On SAMER test the baseline is
\textbf{SARI 77.5 overall} but \textbf{56.1} on the 1{,}678 rows a human actually
simplified. The second number is the bar.

\measured \textbf{Model~1.} On SAMER dev: SARI 77.06 overall and 63.66 on changed
references, against a baseline of 74.25 and 56.21 --- $+2.81$ and $+7.46$. Under
greedy decoding, which is what the on-device build uses, the same model scores
\emph{below} the baseline (\autoref{fig:sari}). Beam search accounts for the
difference; whether to pay its cost on the device is pending a latency
measurement.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\figsari
\caption{SARI on 300 matched SAMER dev rows, same model, same rows. Reported
against a copy-the-input baseline, the gain is modest; under the greedy decoder
we ship, it is negative. Scored with the \texttt{evaluate} SARI implementation
\cite{sari}, reproduced independently.}
\label{fig:sari}
\end{figure}

\measured \textbf{Behavioural findings.} The model copies its input 66.5\% of the
time against a human rate of 41.2\%, and returns unchanged text on 46.8\% of the
rows a human simplified. Where it edits, 75.3\% of edits change a single word
(humans: 53.2\%), and the median prediction-to-source length ratio is
\textbf{1.000} --- it substitutes vocabulary but never restructures a sentence.
\autoref{fig:training} shows why: the selection metric flattened while the
behaviour we care about was still improving.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\figtraining
\caption{Training dynamics for model~1. Copy rate falls monotonically from
91.4\% and has not converged at the final step, while SARI --- the metric that
selected the checkpoint --- is flat inside noise after step 1{,}800. Validation
loss bottoms near step 2{,}200 and then rises.}
\label{fig:training}
\end{figure}

\measured \textbf{Out-of-domain collapse.} On diacritized BAREC input, which the
training data never contained, only 60\% of source words survive into the output
and 38\% of sentences lose more than half their content to character-level
corruption; on undiacritized input the figures are 90\% and 3\%
(\autoref{fig:ood}). This is why diacritization must run \emph{after}
simplification in \autoref{fig:arch}, never before.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\figood
\caption{Out-of-domain behaviour on 300 BAREC test sentences, greedy decoding.
The model was trained on SAMER, which contains no diacritics; 56\% of BAREC
does.}
\label{fig:ood}
\end{figure}

\measured \textbf{Meaning errors are not rare.} 832 of the model's substitutions
disagree with the human reference, 347 of them on words the human deliberately
left alone. Observed examples include \ar{عزلة} (isolation) $\rightarrow$
\ar{استعباد} (enslavement) and \ar{ملجم} (bridled) $\rightarrow$ \ar{مسلح}
(armed). Neither SARI nor BLEU detects this class of error, which is why
BERTScore \cite{bertscore} and human error annotation are part of the
evaluation.

\measured \textbf{Diacritization.} Four models were benchmarked on the pinned
Tashkeela test split (\autoref{tab:diac}). Libtashkeel was selected. CATT is
statistically tied on accuracy --- the 0.01 point difference is about six
character positions across 67{,}576 --- but sixteen times larger and six times
slower. CAMeL Tools was rejected on a stronger ground than accuracy: it altered
base letters in 32\% of sentences, corrupting text before the simplifier sees
it.

\subsection{User Interface}

Selecting Arabic text in any application shows \ar{تبسيط} in the system menu.
Choosing it opens a modal bottom sheet over the host application with the
original text in a muted card and the simplified text below, plus controls for
read-aloud, copy and settings.

Typography is set for dyslexic reading rather than density: line height 1.6,
additional line spacing 10\,sp, letter spacing 0.04, and right-to-left text left
\emph{unjustified}, because justification in Arabic stretches words and opens
uneven rivers of space. Selection behaviour was verified across Chrome,
WhatsApp, Google Keep, Gmail and PDF readers.

\subsection{Testing and Improvements}

41 unit and Robolectric tests cover intent extraction, empty-input handling,
view binding and the typography attributes. The leakage checker is tested
against a deliberately planted duplicate and must exit non-zero. Running the
scoring script end to end on real data --- rather than only on fixtures ---
exposed a bug that placed every BAREC row in the unchanged-reference bucket
instead of a separate no-reference bucket, since BAREC has no gold
simplifications at all.

Improvements already made as a result of measurement: the copy baseline is
reported beside every score; checkpoint selection moved to the
changed-reference subset under greedy decoding; diacritics are stripped before
the simplifier; and 9.3\% of a published corpus was removed before it could
contaminate our results.

% ========================================================== 4 Projected Impact
\section{Projected Impact}

\subsection{Accomplishments and Benefits}

\measured To date: a copy-the-input baseline on two locked test sets; a first
model trained and scored against it with its failure modes characterised; a
four-model diacritization benchmark; and 9.3\% test-set contamination detected
and removed from a published corpus before training.

\textbf{Built:} a working Android plugin shell on the \texttt{PROCESS\_TEXT}
intent; a locked and hashed test-set manifest; a reusable leakage checker; and a
scoring pipeline that reports a copy baseline beside every metric.

\textbf{Benefits.} Reading independence at the moment and place the difficulty
occurs, rather than in a separate application. Support for students, teachers
and schools, and for readers with low literacy and learners of Arabic.
Everything runs on the device, so the tool works offline and the reader's text
stays private.

\textbf{For the field:} an Arabic simplification corpus, an evaluation benchmark
with human-written references, and a leakage-detection tool, none of which
existed in openly reusable form.

\subsection{Future Improvements}

\planned Before submission: complete the mixed-corpus runs and choose between
AraT5v2 and AraBART on measured results; quantize the winner and measure size
and latency on a real phone; run the human evaluation with Arabic readers who
have dyslexia; report annotator agreement.

\textbf{Known limitations.} The current model substitutes vocabulary but does
not restructure sentences, and clause splitting is arguably the larger part of
the problem for dyslexic readers. Automatic metrics do not detect
meaning-altering substitutions, so human annotation remains necessary.
On-device latency is not yet measured, so the choice between greedy and beam
decoding is open.

\textbf{Beyond the capstone.} Fine-tune the diacritizer on simplified output
rather than using it unchanged; add read-aloud with word-level timing; and
extend the corpus beyond the three domains currently covered.
