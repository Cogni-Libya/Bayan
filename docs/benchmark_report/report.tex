\begin{abstract}\noindent
We compare \NSystems{} systems: copying the input, the two models in today's app, models~1 and~2, the model~3 family
(AraT5v2 trained on corpus~v1) with every decoding, precision and safety-net variant, a fluency retrain of model~3, and
two dropped attempts (student DPO and minimum-risk training). All are scored with the frozen BayanBench~v2.0 rules on
the dev and test splits, with meaning judged by Gemma~4~31B (AUC~\JudgeAUC{} against the human majority). The
candidate to ship, \ship, is \emph{at least as faithful} as today's app on test (P(same) \TestShipPsame{} vs
\TestAppPsame; paired difference \TestDiffPsame, an interval that just touches zero), keeps every number
(\TestShipNum\% vs \TestAppNum\%), changes no negation, limit or condition, and leaves easy and protected text alone far
more often. It shortens the longest clause clearly more (\TestShipClause{} vs \TestAppClause{} words; paired
\TestDiffClause), but its reading-level drop is not significantly larger on test (\TestShipLevel{} vs
\TestAppLevel{}). Two caveats run through the report: int8 quantization costs about half of model~3's float
simplification, and the ship candidate reaches its meaning score partly by leaving more text unchanged
(\TestShipUnch\% vs \TestAppUnch\%); on the outputs it does change, P(same) is \TestShipPsameChanged{} vs
\TestAppPsameChanged{} for the app.
\end{abstract}

\section{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Recommendation: ship \ship{}} (\texttt{Congi-libya/bayan-arat5-v1-bundle}, 471\,MB): the best balance we
  have. Against today's app it is never significantly worse on meaning, significantly better on every safety measure,
  and significantly simpler on clause length on both splits (Table~\ref{tab:gate}, Figure~\ref{fig:gate}).
\item \textbf{Test, core items, paired against today's app:} P(same) \TestDiffPsame; meaning kept \TestDiffKept{}
  points; clause \TestDiffClause{} words; reading level \TestDiffLevel{} levels; numbers kept \TestShipNum\% vs
  \TestAppNum\%; easy text left unchanged \TestShipEasy\% vs \TestAppEasy\%.
\item \textbf{Dev agrees and is a little stronger:} P(same) \DevDiffPsame; clause \DevDiffClause{} words; level
  \DevDiffLevel{} (significant on dev).
\item \textbf{The cost of the phone:} float model~3 with the net shortens the longest clause by \TestMnetClause{} words
  and drops \TestMnetLevel{} levels; the int8 bundle with beam~4 and the net, \TestShipClause{} and
  \TestShipLevel{}. Beam~4 and the net buy back meaning, not simplicity (Figure~\ref{fig:factorial}).
\item \textbf{Copying inflates P(same):} a copy counts as perfect meaning. The ship candidate leaves \TestShipUnch\% of
  core test items unchanged (app: \TestAppUnch\%). On the outputs it does change, its P(same) is
  \TestShipPsameChanged{} vs \TestAppPsameChanged{} for the app's (Figure~\ref{fig:violins}): when it rewrites, it
  rewrites harder and loses more meaning per rewrite. The net and the scorer catch the dangerous cases (numbers,
  negations), not every omission.
\item \textbf{The fluency retrain is not worth shipping:} small gains in Gemma's Arabic score, more copying, and under
  int8 it simplifies less than model~3.
\item \textbf{Dropped:} student DPO (better proxies, worse on the 31B scorer) and MRT (the judge in the loss made
  outputs simpler but not more faithful).
\item \textbf{int8 needs a VNNI-class CPU:} the app's per-channel int8 recipe gives nonsense on an AVX2-only Xeon and
  fluent output on an AVX-VNNI CPU; ARM phones use the same integer dot-product path as VNNI (Table~\ref{tab:int8}),
  but this still has to be confirmed on a phone.
\end{itemize}

\section{Benchmark, scorer and statistics}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{BayanBench v2.0} (frozen 4 Oct; data v1.0, 731 dev and 1,250 test items). Item sets:
  \emph{core} (BAREC sentences), \emph{held-out} (news and legal), \emph{outside} (real text from outside BAREC and
  SAMER) and \emph{written} (medicine leaflets). Tracks: A1--A3 simplify, B hard text, C easy text (should stay), E1--E4
  edge cases (cut-off endings, lists, dialogue), F protected text (scripture).
\item \textbf{Meaning} is Gemma~4~31B (int4) in scoring mode: P(yes) for \emph{same}, \emph{added}, \emph{missing}
  and \emph{contradict}. The official rule: \emph{meaning kept} = P(same) $\ge 0.5$ and every number kept.
\item \textbf{Simpler} is measured as change: words cut from the longest clause (items with a clause over 15 words),
  BAREC reading levels dropped by the hardest sentence (items at level 10+), and SAMER level-4/5 words removed.
\item \textbf{Joint} (meaning kept and simpler) uses our own minimum change, 2 clause words or half a level; v2.0
  leaves it undecided.
\item \textbf{Extra questions} (diagnostic): Gemma~31B's P(yes) for ``Arabic correct'', ``easier to read'' and
  ``coherent''. Their agreement with raters is weak (Section~\ref{sec:judge}), so they are never used for decisions.
\item \textbf{Intervals} are 95\% cluster-bootstrap intervals over documents (bayanbench's own \texttt{stats.ci});
  paired differences use the same items for both systems. In tables, $\uparrow$/$\downarrow$ mark paired differences
  whose interval excludes zero.
\end{itemize}

\section{Can we trust the scorer?}
\label{sec:judge}
Six raters rated 290 tasks in the BayanBench v2 round (3 Oct): 245 single outputs (meaning, Arabic, ease) and 45
side-by-side pairs. Table~\ref{tab:judge} and Figures~\ref{fig:confusion}--\ref{fig:raters} compare the scorer with the
human majority.
\begin{table}[H]\centering\small\caption{The scorer against human raters.}\label{tab:judge}\tab{judge}{}\end{table}
\fig{confusion}{Confusion matrices against the human majority (rows; ``tie'' = no majority). Counts and row
percentages.}
\fig{judge_roc}{Left: ROC of P(same) (and of the MiMo second-judge pilot) for the human majority ``same''. Middle: how
accuracy, balanced accuracy, same-kept rate and changes-caught rate move with the threshold. Right: calibration.}
\fig{raters}{Agreement between raters, and of each rater with the scorer (binary: same vs changed).}
\noindent\textbf{Reading.} The meaning scorer separates ``same'' from ``changed'' well (AUC \JudgeAUC), and the
frozen 0.5 threshold gives a balanced accuracy close to the best cut's (0.79 at 0.5 vs 0.81 at 0.30; Table~\ref{tab:judge}): at 0.5 the scorer lets about a quarter of the human-``same'' pairs fail, so it errs towards strictness. Humans agree only moderately with one another on meaning
($\alpha$ = \AlphaMeaning{} binary), and barely on Arabic ($\alpha$ = \AlphaArabic) and ease ($\alpha$ =
\AlphaEase): this is why the extra questions stay diagnostic. MiMo failed the second-judge rule (it sided with the
humans on only half of Gemma's disagreements).

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One scorer.} Meaning rests on Gemma~4~31B (AUC \JudgeAUC{} against humans); the second-judge pilot
  failed, so there is no independent check yet. The extra questions are diagnostic only.
\item \textbf{Copies count as perfect meaning.} Systems that copy more look more faithful; we report unchanged rates
  and P(same) on changed outputs alongside.
\item \textbf{The joint rate uses our own minimum change} (2 clause words or half a level); v2.0 leaves it open.
\item \textbf{Human ratings predate model~3.} The rating round covered the app and model~2 only.
\item \textbf{int8 was checked on two x86 CPUs, not on a phone.} ARM uses the same integer dot-product path as VNNI,
  but a phone latency and output check is still needed.
\item \textbf{Latency numbers come from different machines} (Table~\ref{tab:device}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproducing this report}
\texttt{docs/benchmark\_report/README.md} explains how to rebuild every system's outputs, the Gemma~31B scores and the
scorecards from scratch. With the outputs and score files in place:
{\footnotesize\begin{verbatim}
export BAYANBENCH_DATA=<snapshot of Congi-libya/bayanbench-data>   # v1.0 data
export BAYAN_DATA=<repo>/data/processed/meaning_reward              # system outputs and score files
export SAMER_DIR=<licensed SAMER copy>                              # hard-word measures (optional)
python collect.py   # official v2.0 scorecards, every system and split
make                # analyse.py -> figures.py -> tables.py -> latexmk
\end{verbatim}}
